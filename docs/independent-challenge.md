# Independent challenge of consequential findings

Reviewed independently by a second research agent on **2026-10-17**. Scope: challenge the proposed India-based, fictional adult AI influencer and paid explicit-content model. No persona or content was created; no provider was contacted. This is a preliminary research review, not a legal opinion.

## Findings that must survive challenge

1. **AI disclosure and fictional adulthood do not establish legality in India.** Confirmed statutory text: IT Act sections 67 and 67A concern electronic publication/transmission of obscene material and sexually explicit acts/conduct. **Interpretation:** their language supplies no general exemption for fictional AI adults, foreign subscribers, or paywalls. However, claiming that every nude image necessarily violates section 67A overstates the text: that provision specifies acts/conduct; obscenity under section 67 requires its own analysis. The public-good/art proviso is conditional, not an automatic artistic-content exemption. No controlling judgment deciding the exact proposed synthetic-content model was independently located.

2. **The 2026 synthetic-information amendments are operative, not merely an October 2025 proposal.** Official rules contain G.S.R. 120(E), dated 10 February 2026; the official FAQ identifies commencement on 20 February 2026. Rule 3(3)(a)(i)(I) expressly includes pornographic/sexually explicit SGI in intermediary prevention duties. Rule 3(3)(a)(ii) labeling applies to information outside sub-clause (i). **Interpretation:** labeling cannot be presented as a cure for prohibited synthetic pornography. The rule principally imposes intermediary duties; it should not be described as a standalone criminal penalty for every unlabeled user upload. Exact operator liability and the SGI definition's application to explicitly fictional but realistic imagery require counsel.

3. **Fanvue is a candidate for policy review, not a confirmed India payment route.** Its generic worldwide payout language is expressly location-dependent and contains no India-specific approval. Bank-transfer availability, creator onboarding, acceptance of this content, receiving-bank acceptance, and legal remittance treatment remain separate questions. A third-party directory of purported Indian creators proves none of them.

4. **Patreon is unsuitable for a wholly fictional hyperrealistic adult persona under its current published policy.** It permits animated/illustrated adult AI characters but requires a real consenting adult likeness for hyperrealistic adult depictions. Separately, it bars sexual activity involving real or hyperrealistic participants. Do not collapse these into a claim that all AI adult art is banned.

5. **Fansly expressly rejects photorealistic/lifelike AI content, including disclosed content.** It allows applications by non-photorealistic virtual entities using the operator's real identity. This is a substantive mismatch for the realistic-persona version of the proposal; it does not establish India payout eligibility for an illustrated alternative.

6. **OnlyFans remains unverified in this independent pass.** Its own terms URL failed automated retrieval. Agency copies of a purported help answer say AI content must feature the verified creator, but those are not primary evidence. Do not classify a fictional persona as approved or quote the agency copy as OnlyFans-confirmed. Obtain accessible current primary terms/help text or written provider confirmation before a definitive suitability claim.

7. **Instagram's adult funnel cannot be assumed compliant merely because its feed is clothed.** Meta's own 2024 response to the US Senate says its Facebook/Instagram policies prohibit offers/requests for pornography, including external pornographic links, and apply to AI. Current Transparency Center retrieval failed in this independent pass, so this older primary statement supports a serious funnel risk, not an exhaustive October 2026 ruling about every subscription-platform URL. Avoid asserting that any Fanvue link is categorically banned regardless of destination/content; avoid treating a link aggregator as permission or a workaround.

8. **PayPal requires jurisdiction-specific care.** Independently retrieved India AUP prohibits unlawful, obscene and certain sexually oriented transactions, but its preapproval table also lists digitally delivered mature content. This prevents an unqualified claim, from this India page alone, that every adult digital transaction is universally forbidden; it equally prevents treating the row as approval. **Decision:** no approved PayPal route has been established. Obtain clarification of the exact content, account jurisdiction and product, reconciling stricter help-center statements with the India AUP.

## Required corrections and unresolved gates

- Replace any statement that fictional AI pornography is legally safe with an unresolved, consequential India legal-risk finding.
- Replace claims that AI labels authorize adult SGI with the separate prevention/labeling analysis.
- Mark Fanvue India onboarding and payouts **unconfirmed**, even if the platform accepts AI content generally.
- Mark realistic fictional adult AI **incompatible** with Patreon/Fansly's cited policy texts; preserve the distinction for illustrated content.
- Mark OnlyFans primary policy verification **incomplete** in this pass.
- Treat Instagram adult promotion as a separate gate; feed compliance cannot establish destination-link compliance or recommendation eligibility.
- A launch decision requires a written Indian legal assessment and provider/bank confirmations for the exact operator, content category, and payout route. These are evidence requirements for feasibility, not permission inferred from legal or platform silence.
